A high-resolution archival museum inventory photograph of a small Russian Orthodox liturgical censer (kadilo, approximately 20 cm tall), photographed at a three-quarter elevated angle on a dark forest-green velvet cloth, in the documentary style of Russian rare-book department catalog photography.

The censer is hand-cast bronze, darkened by decades of liturgical use: warm patinated metal, soot residue inside the bowl, fine scratches and dents from handling, no display polishing. The lid is a small onion-dome topped with a Russian Orthodox cross; pierced openwork for incense smoke. Three fine, slightly tarnished bronze chains converge to a single ring handle.

Around the widest part of the bowl runs a continuous band of small engraved oval portrait medallions in low relief, each about 1.5 cm tall — hand-engraved miniature philosophical portraits with the slight irregularity of an inspired non-professional master: a 20th-century European philosopher with high forehead and brushed-back hair (Heidegger); an Orthodox priest with round wire-rim glasses and long beard (Florensky); an 18th-century figure in powdered wig with a thin alert face (Kant); a stocky Ukrainian wanderer in fur cap with a staff (Skovoroda); a medieval Dominican monk in hood with downcast eyes (Eckhart); plus three or four further unidentified male profiles. The medallions bear no name labels.

Below the medallion band, around the base of the bowl, a single line of carefully hand-engraved Cyrillic text in pre-revolutionary Russian orthography (with the letters Ъ — hard sign, Ѣ — yat, and І — decimal i clearly and accurately rendered): «ПОСТАВЛЕНО ВЪ ДАРЪ АЛАКАЕВСКОЙ ОБИТЕЛИ. Н. 1948». The lettering must be precise, legible, evenly spaced, with the slight irregularity of hand-engraving.

On the velvet alongside the censer: a small folded archival paper tag tied to one of the chains with thin cotton string, hand-written in faded purple ink in old-Russian cursive, reading «Архивъ Петра-Дубравской школы. Lighting: soft, warm, diffuse, slightly raking from upper left, museum-quality, no harsh reflections — designed to keep both the inscription and the medallion portraits legible while preserving the dark depth of the velvet background. Color palette: deep forest green of the velvet, warm patinated bronze of the censer, faded sepia of the paper tag, white of the glove and ruler markings.

Photographic style: medium-format documentary archival inventory photography in the tradition of Russian rare-book and museum departments — straightforward objective documentation, no bokeh, no artistic blur, no dramatic effects, sharp focus on the inscription and engravings, slightly desaturated, with the subtle natural grain of fine-grained film.
