A high-resolution archival documentation photograph of a damaged 
silver-gelatin glass photographic plate from the early 20th century, 
displayed on a softly illuminated lightbox in a museum conservation 
setting. The plate measures 18 × 24 cm and is a positive lantern 
slide showing a black-and-white photographic image of the interior 
of a small wooden Russian Orthodox church.

THE IMAGE ON THE PLATE, illuminated from beneath through the 
lightbox and clearly legible in neutral grey tones, shows: the 
dim interior of a wooden village church with a low timbered ceiling 
and dark wooden floorboards. In the center of the composition, 
raised on a small step before the iconostasis, stands a large 
carved and gilded icon-case (kiot) containing a single prominent 
icon. The icon is large — approximately 110 × 150 cm in apparent 
size — and shows a frontal standing winged angel with a golden 
halo, holding an open pair of draftsman's dividers in the right 
hand and an unfurled scroll in the left. The angel's face is 
solemn, with elongated almond eyes. A heavy chased-silver oklad 
covers most of the figure, leaving only the face, hands, and the 
scroll visible; the oklad catches the candle-light. Around the 
central image, twelve small narrative scenes (kleima) are arranged 
along the four sides of the icon, visible as small framed squares 
— their details slightly blurred by photographic distance but 
clearly present in a regular border.

SURROUNDING THE ICON IN THE CHURCH: a tall brass floor candlestand 
holding multiple lit beeswax candles before the kiot, casting soft 
highlights on the silver oklad; an oil lamp (lampada) suspended on 
a thin chain from above; embroidered linen rushniks draped over 
the side posts of the icon-case; several small silver votive 
offerings (embossed plaques representing healed limbs, hearts, and 
eyes) hanging on fine chains across the lower edge of the kiot. 
Before the kiot stands a wooden lectern (analoy) with an open 
large-format service book in pre-revolutionary Church Slavonic 
typography.

Behind the kiot, parts of the wooden iconostasis are visible: the 
lower local tier with several darker icons of saints in simpler 
frames; above them, partly cut off by the photograph's edge, the 
Royal Doors (Tsarskie Vrata) with carved gilded ornament; faint 
silhouettes of icons in the upper tiers fading into the dim church 
interior. Two small windows on the right side of the church admit 
weak afternoon light, illuminating the dust in the air with faint 
diagonal beams.

CONDITION OF THE PLATE: a single long crack runs from the upper 
left corner diagonally across the middle of the image down to the 
lower right edge, dividing the plate into two fragments that fit 
together precisely (the fragments are held in registration by two 
thin strips of beige archival paper tape applied along the back, 
faintly visible through the glass). The lower right corner shows 
a small additional triangular chip missing. The silver-gelatin 
emulsion has flaked off in two small patches: one near the upper 
register obscuring part of the iconostasis, one along the right 
margin obscuring part of the window. Several long fine scratches 
run vertically through the emulsion. The glass itself has the 
slightly greenish tint typical of period plates.
