A high-resolution photograph of an 18th-century Russian Orthodox 
wooden icon, in the style of archival museum documentation. 
The icon is a tempera-on-gessoed-wood panel, approximately 
30 × 38 cm, with two horizontal shponka cradle bars implied. 
Photographed top-down on dark green archival velvet, evenly lit 
with soft museum lighting. A white cotton curator's glove is 
partly visible at the lower right corner of the frame. A wooden 
ruler with millimeter markings runs along the bottom edge.

CENTRAL COMPOSITION: a standing winged angel in frontal hieratic 
pose, painted in the naive, austere manner of mid-18th-century 
provincial Russian icon painting (Volga region, possibly Old 
Believer skit school). The angel has an elongated solemn face 
with large almond-shaped dark eyes, ochre flesh tones; a wide 
darkened gold-leaf halo, heavily worn at the edges. Two large 
upright wings rise behind the shoulders, painted in muted 
grey-green tones with stylized feather patterns. The angel wears 
a long pale blue under-tunic and a deeper indigo mantle falling 
in flat, stylized vertical folds with thin gold highlights.

ATTRIBUTES: In the angel's right hand, held vertically: an open 
pair of draftsman's dividers (compass) — an unusual iconographic 
attribute. In the left hand, held outward and slightly unfurled: 
a parchment scroll showing a naive geometric diagram — two 
perpendicular axes crossing at right angles, with eight small 
painted star-marks distributed asymmetrically across the four 
quadrants around the central crossing. The diagram is rendered 
in the flat, schematic manner of an icon painter copying a 
figure he does not fully understand.

UPPER REGISTER: emerging from a dark blue segment of sky with 
small white stylized stars, the blessing right hand of God 
(manus Dei) in the Byzantine convention, framed in a small 
semicircle.

LOWER REGISTER: beneath the angel's feet, a small panoramic 
strip showing a Russian village — log isbas with mossy plank 
roofs, a small wooden chapel with a single dark onion dome, 
a grove of tall oaks, and a meandering river line — rendered 
in the same flat naive perspective.

INSCRIPTIONS in pre-revolutionary Church Slavonic with titlos:
— in cinnabar-red lettering across the top: 
  «СВѦТЫЙ АНГЕЛЪ АЛАКАЕВСКIЙ»
— in finer black lettering along the lower edge: 
  «ЯВИСѦ В ЛѢТО 1747-Е IЮНѦ ВЪ 9-Й ДЕНЬ»

CONDITION: the panel shows deep age cracks running vertically; 
significant loss of paint surface revealing pale gesso 
underground in several patches; candle smoke darkening especially 
in the upper third; two small woodworm holes near the lower edge; 
the gold background heavily oxidized to a dark olive-brown tone 
with extensive craquelure; the lower border bears traces of an 
earlier, partly effaced inscription.

ARCHIVAL CONTEXT: To the upper-left of the icon on the velvet, 
a small ivory-colored archival cardstock card with typewritten 
Cyrillic text in pre-revolutionary orthography:

«Отдѣлъ Рѣдкой Книги. Инв. № 8742.
Икона. „Святый Ангелъ Алакаевскій".
Дерево, темпера. XVIII в.
Происхожденіе: с. Алакаевка, Самарск. губ.
Поступила въ 1924 г. изъ собранія
іеромонаха Никодима. Не атрибутирована.»

Two purple oval library stamps overlap the lower edge of the 
card: one of the Imperial University of Samara library, 
one of the Department of Rare Books.

STYLE: matter-of-fact museum conservation photograph; even 
neutral lighting; no dramatic shadows; the gloss of old varnish 
faintly reflects in one corner of the gold ground; 
fine resolution; no artistic stylization of the photograph 
itself — only the icon is stylized, its photograph is plain.
