Photorealistic archival photograph, shot from directly above on a dark grey-brown cloth surface, soft directional daylight from the upper left, shallow natural shadows. Portrait format 2:3. The image shows a case file from a fictional state archive, photographed the way museums photograph historical documents.

WORLD CONTEXT (for correct interpretation, do not depict literally):
The documents come from the R.S.F.G.R. («Р.С.Ф.Г.Р.»), a fictional alternate Russia of the 1920s–30s in which the revolutionary state merged with a Lovecraftian cult: Lenin is "the Sleeper" beneath the Mausoleum, the capital is the city of R'lyeh on the Kara Sea, and citizens are ceremonially "dissolved" into the Great Mycelium (Великая Грибница), a collective fungal organism; after dissolution a family receives "one spore" instead of the person. Individual reason is treated as a crime ("рассудок в единоличном владении"); citizens may wear glass jars (сосуды) on their heads as external containers for their reason. The ministry in charge is the People's Commissariat of Healthy Madness (Наркоматъ Здороваго Безумія). Its emblem is a red five-pointed star with a spiral in the centre instead of a hammer and sickle; its round violet rubber stamp shows an octopus-like figure. Years are counted in "Sporolétie" (Споролѣтіе), year I = 1921, so Споролѣтіе XVI = 1936. "The dry world" (сухой міръ) means our ordinary, rational, non-mystical world; in this state, stories about the dry world are heretical folklore that frighten people precisely because nothing supernatural happens in them. This case file (Дѣло № 611-С) is an investigation into such folklore — "dry sayings" (сухія присказки) — found at a soil-moisture research institute, НИИ-27 «Мицелій». The mood of the image must be quiet, dry, bureaucratic dread: no monsters, no tentacles, no glowing effects, no fantasy illustration. Everything looks like a real 1930s Soviet-era archive object.
