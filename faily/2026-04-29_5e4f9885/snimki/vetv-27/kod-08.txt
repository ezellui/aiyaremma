Anime poster illustration, full-body magical girl warrior character.
90s Japanese anime aesthetic: clean cel-shaded line art,
large melancholic expressive eyes, disheveled dark hair —
loose, half-pinned, as if forgotten mid-gesture,
pale complexion, slight otherworldly fragility,
long neck, the posture of someone perpetually cold.

Uniform: off-white and black magical warrior outfit,
tarnished gold trim — not shining but oxidized,
structured collar loosely worn, white gloves
slightly dirty at fingertips, knee-high black boots,
one ribbon untied and trailing.

Brooch: a small black sun — radiating darkness instead of light.

Weapon: a long black flag on a staff,
no symbol on the flag, fabric fraying at the edges,
caught in wind from an unknown direction,
the flag casting no shadow.

Expression: not sad — beyond sadness.
Calm, slightly unfocused gaze directed upward and past the viewer.
The face of someone who has seen something beautiful
and understood it will not save her.
Lips slightly parted — as if she just stopped speaking,
or is about to begin.

Background: Paris rooftops at 4am, neither night nor dawn,
rain-slicked cobblestones far below,
cheap cafe light leaking from a window,
scattered torn manuscript pages floating upward,
absinthe-colored haze at the edges,
a single boat drifting on dark water in the distance.
Faint Orthodox cross geometry dissolved into the star field above.

Poster layout: large vertical format,
title at bottom in worn, slightly uneven gold lettering:
"SAILOR ABANDON"
subtitle beneath in smaller French italic script:
"Gardienne de la Nuit Sans Réponse"
— Guardian of the Night Without Answer.

Color palette: off-white, matte black, tarnished gold,
pre-dawn Paris blue, absinthe green at margins,
no bright colors, no sparkle — only diffuse cold glow.

Style: vintage Japanese magical girl anime illustration,
detailed linework, melancholic dramatic lighting,
Montparnasse decadent atmosphere, collectible poster composition.
Small detail: a half-empty absinthe glass barely visible at her feet,
and a single white rose — three days past cutting.
