A vertical philosophical poster in the same style as a 17th-century Dutch 
tenebrist engraving crossed with anime sentai illustration. Title at the top 
in elegant serif capitals: "SAILOR COGITO", with subtitle below: "WITNESS OF 
THE DOUBTING MIND". Beneath the subtitle, a smaller Latin motto: "DUBITO ERGO 
SUM".

Central figure: a young woman standing in a quiet, decisive pose — one hand 
raised pensively to her temple, the other extended forward holding a small 
piece of pale wax that is visibly softening and changing shape between her 
fingers. Her gaze is directed STRAIGHT AT THE VIEWER, calm, intense, 
self-aware. She is the only thing in the entire image that is rendered with 
absolute sharpness and full opacity.

Palette: deep black background, cold ivory and pale silver as primary colors, 
muted mercury-grey accents, NO gold, NO red, NO bright color. Lighting is 
single-source, candlelit, in the manner of Honthorst or early Rembrandt — a 
warm point of light somewhere off-frame illuminating only the figure, the 
rest of the scene falling into chiaroscuro darkness.

CRITICAL DESIGN: the world around the figure is UNSTABLE and visibly under 
suspicion. The architecture behind her — a dim 17th-century study with 
shelves, a stove, an astrolabe, a globe — partially dissolves at the edges, 
doubles, blurs, melts as if seen in a dream. Some objects appear twice in 
slightly different positions, as if the viewer cannot trust whether they are 
really there. The figure herself, by contrast, is razor-sharp, fully present, 
the only thing in the image that is certainly real.

A faint translucent CARTESIAN COORDINATE GRID is projected over the entire 
scene, glowing softly in pale silver — it is sharper and more confident around 
the figure, fainter and broken at the dissolving edges of the room. This is 
the order that reason imposes on the doubtful world.

Costume: a sailor-senshi-style uniform in austere ivory and dark charcoal, 
high collar, modest cuffs, a dark half-cape lined with pale silver. A 
medallion at the chest showing a single open EYE inside an open book — the 
eye of the meditating mind. Boots dark, simple, scholarly rather than 
ornamental.

Tiara: a slender silver circlet over the brow with a single small clear 
flame-shaped crystal at the center, glowing faintly from within (the lumen 
naturale, the natural light of reason).

In her left hand: a soft pale piece of wax, partially melting, dripping. 
Tiny inscription floating beside it in Latin: "Sed quid est hoc quod tam 
distincte percipio?" ("but what is this that I perceive so distinctly?").

In the upper-left or upper-right shadows of the background, BARELY VISIBLE: 
the silhouette of a tall hooded figure in deep black with no visible face — 
the EVIL GENIUS (malin génie) — its presence felt rather than seen, watching, 
half-dissolved into the darkness. Tiny inscription beneath it: "GENIUS 
MALIGNUS".

Border composition — the LADDER OF DOUBT: instead of a temporal sequence of 
epochs, the entire frame is structured as a vertical descent and ascent of 
six meditations on each side, twelve panels total.

Left column, descending from top to bottom (the levels of doubt):
- "MEDITATIO I — DE SENSIBUS" — a hand reaching into water and the stick 
  appearing bent (the senses deceive).
- "MEDITATIO I — DE SOMNIIS" — a sleeping figure dreaming of waking; the 
  question of whether one is awake.
- "MEDITATIO I — DE MATHESI" — geometric figures (a triangle, a circle) 
  flickering, as even mathematical truths come under suspicion.
- "MEDITATIO I — DE GENIO MALIGNO" — a shadowy hooded figure casting 
  illusions; the hypothesis of the deceiving god.
- "MEDITATIO II — TENEBRAE" — a dark void with nothing visible.
- "MEDITATIO II — UNUM PUNCTUM" — at the bottom of the descent, a single 
  small point of light in total darkness, labeled "EGO SUM, EGO EXISTO".

Right column, ascending from bottom to top (the levels of recovered 
certainty):
- "MEDITATIO II — RES COGITANS" — a luminous mind-flame above an empty body 
  outline.
- "MEDITATIO II — CERA" — the same piece of wax in solid and melted form, 
  recognized by intellect alone.
- "MEDITATIO III — IDEA DEI" — a radiant infinite circle, the idea of a 
  perfect being inscribed in a finite mind.
- "MEDITATIO IV — VERUM ET FALSUM" — a balanced scale of will and intellect.
- "MEDITATIO V — DEUS NON FALLIT" — a serene divine eye, no longer the 
  deceiver but the guarantor of clarity.
- "MEDITATIO VI — RES EXTENSA" — at the top of the ascent, the world restored: 
  geometric solids, a human body, a landscape, all clearly delineated.

The two columns must visually mirror each other — descent on the left, ascent 
on the right — so the eye reads the poster as a U-shaped journey: from the 
intact world, down into total doubt at the bottom, and back up to a world 
restored on certain foundations.

Bottom corners — two small historical scenes in oval frames:
- Bottom left: "LA FLÈCHE / ULM, 1619" — a young man in a small heated room 
  (poêle), alone at a table by a stove, in the night of his three famous 
  dreams; a vague halo of dream-images above his head.
- Bottom right: "STOCKHOLM, 1650" — an older man in a cold palace giving 
  philosophy lessons to Queen Christina at five in the morning; frosted 
  windows, candles, the chill almost visible.

Bottom of the poster, in elegant serif capitals: "COGITO ERGO SUM". Below it 
in smaller italic Latin: "DE OMNIBUS DUBITANDUM EST". Bottommost banner 
across the full width, small caps: "ARCHIMEDIS PUNCTUM — UNUM QUOD CERTUM 
EST: ME ESSE".

Style: tenebrist 17th-century engraving aesthetic, photorealistic anime 
figure design, austere cold palette dominated by black, ivory, and silver, 
candlelit chiaroscuro, ornate but restrained silver filigree frame (no gold), 
vertical poster format, dense composition, museum print quality, atmosphere 
of nocturnal study and lucid intellectual solitude.
