A vertical baroque philosophical poster in the same style as a 17th-century 
Dutch frontispiece crossed with anime sentai illustration. Title at the top in 
large serif capitals: "SAILOR SUBSTANZ", with subtitle below: "THE THINKER OF 
THE ONE". Beneath the subtitle, a smaller Latin motto: "DEUS SIVE NATURA".

Central figure: a young woman standing in absolute frontal symmetry, perfectly 
still, with a calm, affectless expression and pale almost colorless eyes 
gazing into infinity (sub specie aeternitatis). She wears a sailor-senshi-style 
uniform but in a radically cold palette: matte white, crystalline translucent 
elements, very cold pale blue shadows, NO gold, NO red. The lighting is even, 
diffuse, Vermeer-like — light seems to come from within the figure rather than 
from outside.

CRITICAL DESIGN: the figure must read as bisected vertically into two halves 
representing the two known attributes of substance:
- Left half (attribute of EXTENSION): fully physical, anatomical, with realistic 
  fabric folds, weight, shadow, baroque tactility.
- Right half (attribute of THOUGHT): the same body rendered as a luminous 
  geometric diagram — Euclidean construction lines on the skin, the silhouette 
  decomposed into axes, circles, and golden-ratio guides, fabric reduced to 
  vector fields and tangent lines.
Both halves must align perfectly: each fold on the left has a geometric 
counterpart on the right. The viewer cannot decide which half is primary.

Tiara: a thin silver circlet with a single large octahedral clear crystal of 
Iceland spar (calcite) above the brow, showing visible double refraction — one 
object seen as two through the stone.

In her hands: an open book ("Ethica") held forward; the pages are mirror-like 
and reflect both the figure and a ghostly landscape behind her. Tiny Latin 
inscriptions along the page edges: "Per substantiam intelligo id quod in se 
est et per se concipitur".

Costume details: high collar, sober cuffs, no flamboyant cape. A small medallion 
at the chest showing a single eye inside an equilateral triangle inscribed in a 
circle — but rendered austere, geometric, not occult.

Border composition (replacing historical panels): the entire poster is framed 
as a "more geometrico" treatise. Around the central figure, instead of 
historical scenes, are small ornate cartouches arranged in vertical columns on 
the left and right, each containing a numbered proposition in Latin in elegant 
calligraphy:
- Top left: "DEFINITIO I — Per causam sui intelligo id cuius essentia involvit 
  existentiam"
- Below it: "AXIOMA I — Omnia quae sunt, vel in se, vel in alio sunt"
- Then: "PROPOSITIO VII — Ad naturam substantiae pertinet existere"
- And: "PROPOSITIO XIV — Praeter Deum nulla dari neque concipi potest substantia"
On the right column similarly:
- "PROPOSITIO XXIX — In rerum natura nullum datur contingens"
- "PROPOSITIO XXXIII — Res nullo alio modo neque alio ordine a Deo produci 
  potuerunt quam productae sunt"
- "PARS V, PROPOSITIO XXXVI — Mentis amor intellectualis erga Deum est ipse 
  Dei amor quo Deus se ipsum amat"

Bottom corners — two small historical scenes in oval frames:
- Bottom left: "AMSTERDAM, 1656" — a dim synagogue interior with a scroll of 
  cherem (excommunication), candles, dark robed elders.
- Bottom right: "RIJNSBURG" — a quiet sunlit room, a man in plain dark clothes 
  bent over a lens-grinding lathe, polishing optical glass; books and geometric 
  instruments on a table.

Bottom of the poster, in elegant capitals: "DEUS SIVE NATURA". Below it in 
smaller italic Latin: "SED OMNIA PRAECLARA TAM DIFFICILIA QUAM RARA SUNT". 
Bottommost banner across the full width, small caps: "ORDO ET CONNECTIO 
IDEARUM IDEM EST AC ORDO ET CONNECTIO RERUM".

Style: photorealistic anime illustration, ornate baroque frame with silver and 
pale-blue filigree (no gold), 17th-century Dutch engraving aesthetic merged 
with senshi figure design, vertical poster format, extremely detailed, dense 
composition, museum print quality.
