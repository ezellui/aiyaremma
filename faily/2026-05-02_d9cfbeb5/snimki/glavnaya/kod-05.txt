A vertical philosophical poster in the style of a quiet, austere German 
Romantic woodblock crossed with anime sentai illustration. Title at the top 
in elegant serif capitals: "SAILOR LICHTUNG", with subtitle below: "THE 
CLEARING OF BEING". Beneath the subtitle, a smaller German motto: "DIE 
SPRACHE IST DAS HAUS DES SEINS".

Central setting: a young woman standing in a CLEARING in a deep Black Forest 
(Schwarzwald) at dawn or dusk. Tall dark spruce and fir trees frame the 
poster on left and right, but the center of the image is OPEN, LUMINOUS, 
EMPTY — a literal Lichtung, a clearing where light enters the forest. The 
empty space around her is not background but the main subject; she is small 
relative to the clearing.

Palette: deep forest greens, warm earth tones (ochre, raw umber, smoky 
brown), weathered grey stone, NO heavy gold, NO red, NO baroque ornament. A 
single warm amber-golden source of light filtering through the canopy from 
above and behind, creating soft volumetric god-rays. Atmosphere of stillness, 
mountain silence, pine resin, cool morning air.

Central figure: a young woman in her late twenties, calm, attentive, 
introspective. She wears a SIMPLE austere version of a sailor-senshi uniform 
adapted to traditional Schwarzwald peasant clothing — coarse linen blouse 
with a plain dark sailor collar, a heavy hand-knit dark wool vest, a simple 
mid-length skirt in dark green wool, sturdy worn leather hiking boots fit 
for forest paths. No ornaments, no filigree, no gold trim. The clothing 
should look like it could actually be worn for walking in the woods.

Hair: long dark hair, a few strands lifted in mountain wind. Tiara: NOT a 
crown, but a simple WREATH of fresh dark green spruce branches with small 
beads of amber resin, woven plainly. Above the wreath, a barely visible thin 
silver halo-ring — a hint of the sacred, not a declaration of it.

CRITICAL POSE AND GESTURE: she stands turned slightly to her right, NOT 
facing the viewer, NOT facing into historical distance, but looking DOWNWARD 
AND INWARD at her own open right palm, which she holds extended in front of 
her at chest level. Her left hand holds a small open book at her side. Her 
expression is one of quiet listening, of attending — Gelassenheit, the 
releasement of attentive waiting. She is the FIFTH and final position of 
gaze in the series, after which there is nowhere else to look.

In her open right palm: NO object. Instead, a soft amber luminescence — a 
flickering golden-warm light hovering above the palm WITHOUT A SOURCE, not 
held but appearing, as if the palm is a place where Being shows itself 
without becoming a thing. The light must clearly NOT be an object — no orb, 
no crystal, no flame, just a luminous presence, a glow with no center.

In her left hand, held loosely at her side: an open small leather-bound book 
of poetry. On the visible page, in elegant German handwritten script, two 
lines from Hölderlin: "Voll Verdienst, doch dichterisch / wohnet der Mensch 
auf dieser Erde."

BORDER COMPOSITION — DAS GEVIERT (the fourfold). Instead of a sequence of 
panels, the entire frame is structured as a four-sided cosmos around the 
clearing:

- TOP EDGE banner labeled "DER HIMMEL" (the sky): a strip showing dawn sky 
  with thin clouds, distant mountain peaks (the Alps far in the distance), 
  and a single faint star.
- BOTTOM EDGE banner labeled "DIE ERDE" (the earth): a strip showing dark 
  forest soil with roots, stones, moss, fallen pine needles, the texture of 
  ground itself.
- LEFT SIDE banner labeled "DIE STERBLICHEN" (the mortals): a vertical strip 
  showing small human figures on a forest path — a peasant with a walking 
  stick, a woman gathering wood, a child, an old man — ordinary mortals 
  going about their lives among the trees.
- RIGHT SIDE banner labeled "DIE GÖTTLICHEN" (the divines): a vertical strip 
  showing faint, half-transparent presences among the trees — old Germanic 
  rune stones, an empty wayside shrine with a candle, a deer that seems 
  almost a vision, a glimpse of something sacred withdrawing.

Inside the four bands, distributed as small ornate cartouches in the corners 
and along the inner edges of the banners, six EXISTENZIALIEN from "Sein und 
Zeit" with brief German labels in elegant serif:
- Top-left corner: "GEWORFENHEIT" — Thrownness — image of a small infant 
  figure suddenly appearing in the forest, alone.
- Top-right corner: "SORGE" — Care — image of two hands cupped protectively 
  around something unseen.
- Mid-left, on the mortals' band: "MITSEIN" — Being-with — silhouettes of 
  several figures walking together on the path.
- Mid-right, on the divines' band: "EIGENTLICHKEIT" — Authenticity — a 
  single figure standing apart from a crowd, facing the open clearing.
- Bottom-left corner: "ANGST" — Anxiety — a void opening at the foot of a 
  cliff, a vertiginous abyss.
- Bottom-right corner: "SEIN-ZUM-TODE" — Being-toward-death — an empty pair 
  of boots on a forest path, a shadow without a body, an hourglass nearly 
  empty.

ANTAGONISTIC ELEMENTS — placed deliberately at the OUTER edges of the 
poster, not at the center:

- Upper-left distant background, just visible BETWEEN the trees: the dim 
  silhouette of an enormous concrete dam or hydroelectric power plant, with 
  high-tension wires cutting across the sky. Tiny label below it: "DAS 
  GESTELL". This is the technological enframing, the modern mode of 
  revealing as standing-reserve.

- Upper-right distant background, just visible: a faceless crowd of 
  identical modern figures in office attire walking in lockstep, blurred 
  and grey. Tiny label: "DAS MAN". This is fallen everyday existence in 
  the impersonal "they".

These two elements are FAR AWAY, on the horizon, small — they should be 
felt as horizons of threat, not present antagonists. The clearing is 
threatened by them but not yet absorbed.

BOTTOM CORNERS — two biographical scenes in oval frames:

- Bottom left, labeled "TODTNAUBERG": a small wooden hut (the famous 
  Hütte) in a Black Forest meadow, smoke from a chimney, a single window 
  glowing warmly at dusk. A simple wooden table inside is faintly visible 
  through the window with papers and a candle. This is Heidegger's 
  retreat, the place of his late thinking.

- Bottom right, labeled "FREIBURG, 1933": a darker, harder scene — a 
  university hall, a man at a lectern in academic robes, banners with 
  ominous symbolism in the background suggested but not explicit. The 
  scene is rendered in colder grey tones than Todtnauberg. This is the 
  rectorship and the political compromise — the catastrophic political 
  moment of the philosophy itself, included honestly rather than concealed.

The two corner scenes form an UNRESOLVED pair: not theory/praxis like 
Hegel, not exile/contemplation like Spinoza, but RETREAT and FALL — the 
two contrary tendencies that the philosophy could not synthesize.

Bottom of the poster, in elegant serif capitals: "DIE SPRACHE IST DAS HAUS 
DES SEINS." Below it in smaller italic: "Nur noch ein Gott kann uns retten." 
Bottommost banner across the full width, in serif small caps with a 
hyphenated Greek phrase: "ἀ-ΛΉΘΕΙΑ — DIE UNVERBORGENHEIT DES SEINS."

Style: austere German woodblock and Romantic landscape painting aesthetic 
(in the tradition of Caspar David Friedrich), photorealistic but quiet 
anime figure design, restrained palette of forest greens and earth tones, 
diffuse god-rays through canopy, vertical poster format, MUCH MORE NEGATIVE 
SPACE than typical baroque posters in the series, atmosphere of mountain 
silence and apophatic patience, museum print quality.
