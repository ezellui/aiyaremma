A vertical philosophical poster in the style of late 20th-century French 
intellectual book covers crossed with anime sentai illustration. Title at 
the top in elegant serif capitals, with the second-to-last letter (A) 
marked in red and slightly raised: "SAILOR DIFFÉRANCE" — note: this is the 
deliberate spelling with an A, NOT "difference" with an E; the A must be 
clearly visible and slightly emphasized. Subtitle below: "THE ONE WHO IS 
NEVER ONE". Beneath the subtitle, a smaller French motto: "IL N'Y A PAS DE 
HORS-TEXTE".

CRITICAL STRUCTURE: instead of a single central figure, the poster contains 
TWO DIFFERENT FIGURES OF SAILOR DIFFÉRANCE, both rendered as the same 
person but in two different settings, neither more central than the other. 
They are separated by a soft semi-transparent diagonal seam running from 
upper-left to lower-right, through which each figure is partially visible 
in the other's space — they overlap and bleed into each other.

LEFT FIGURE — "SAILOR DIFFÉRANCE À PARIS, 1968": a young woman in her late 
twenties, slim, intense expression, dark curly hair worn long. She stands 
in a cluttered French intellectual study at the École Normale Supérieure: 
floor-to-ceiling bookshelves with French and German philosophy (Husserl, 
Heidegger, Hegel, Mallarmé, Lévi-Strauss visible on the spines), a heavy 
wooden desk with an Olivetti typewriter, scattered manuscripts with 
handwritten margin notes in red ink, a single window with grey Parisian 
light. The palette is COOL — slate grey, muted blue, ink black, cream paper.

She wears an austere modernist sailor-senshi uniform in dark navy and 
cream: a clean tailored jacket with a sailor collar, a knee-length pleated 
skirt in dark grey, low boots. Slight intellectual severity. She holds in 
her hands an OPEN BOOK with COMPLETELY BLANK WHITE PAGES. Her face is 
turned to her right, looking ACROSS the diagonal seam toward the other 
figure.

RIGHT FIGURE — "SAILOR DIFFÉRANCE À EL-BIAR, 1942": the same young woman, 
but rendered slightly younger, with shorter hair, less weary, her skin 
warmer-toned from Mediterranean sun. She stands in a sunlit Algerian 
courtyard: whitewashed walls, a single olive tree, a low stone bench, blue 
ceramic tile patterns at the base of the wall, a sliver of bright Algerian 
sky above, a school gate visible in the background but CLOSED to her. The 
palette is WARM — bone white, terracotta, pale gold, sky blue, the dust 
of North African summer.

She wears a softer version of the same sailor uniform in cream and pale 
ochre: a lighter blouse, a simple beige skirt, sandals instead of boots. 
Slight childhood vulnerability. She holds in her hands a SINGLE WRITING 
PEN, held aloft as if mid-stroke, and from the tip of the pen FLOATING 
INK-BLACK LETTERS drift across the diagonal seam toward the LEFT figure's 
blank book — words appear on the left figure's blank pages, as if written 
by the right figure's pen across a distance. The letters spell fragmentary 
French phrases in elegant cursive: "la trace", "il n'y a pas", "tout 
autre…". Her face is turned to her left, looking ACROSS the diagonal seam 
toward the other figure.

CRITICAL GAZE: the two figures look at EACH OTHER across the seam. Their 
gazes meet in the empty zone between them, NOT at the viewer, NOT into 
distance. Each is the other's referent. Neither is the original. This is 
the seventh and final position of gaze in the series: the gaze into one's 
own trace in the other.

In the empty diagonal zone between the two figures, FLOATING LARGE in 
elegant red serif: the word "DIFFÉRANCE" — with the letter A clearly 
emphasized, slightly larger, in deep red ink, while the rest of the word 
is in black. Below the word, in smaller text: "(différance, et non 
différence)".

Each figure wears a small TIARA: the left figure's tiara is a thin silver 
circlet with a CIRCULAR FORM that has a small GAP on its right side; the 
right figure's tiara is the SAME form but with the gap on its LEFT side. 
The two tiaras together would form a complete circle if joined — but they 
never join. The gap is the trace.

LEFT BORDER COLUMN — MARGINALIA OF DECONSTRUCTION (no main text, only 
margin notes, suggesting commentary on a missing center): six small 
ornate cartouches arranged irregularly, each containing one Derridean 
concept rendered as a vignette:
- "LA TRACE" — a small bird's footprint in damp sand, with the wave 
  about to wash it away.
- "LE SUPPLÉMENT" — a scene of Rousseau-era domesticity: a child between 
  a wet nurse and a mother, with arrows pointing in BOTH directions 
  ambiguously, suggesting that "supplement" cannot be assigned a stable 
  origin.
- "LA PHARMAKON" — a small ornate vial containing a liquid that is half 
  golden (medicine) and half dark (poison), un-decidable.
- "LA HANTISE" — a dim empty room with a transparent ghostly silhouette 
  by the window, neither present nor absent.
- "L'ARCHI-ÉCRITURE" — a paleolithic handprint pressed onto a cave wall, 
  the earliest mark.
- "LE DON" — two hands, one giving an unwrapped object, the other 
  receiving — but the object is barely visible, almost transparent, 
  because the gift "becomes a gift" only by NOT being recognized as such.

RIGHT BORDER COLUMN — KEY TEXTS, listed not as a hierarchy but as 
SCATTERED OPEN BOOKS at irregular angles, as if pinned to a corkboard:
- "DE LA GRAMMATOLOGIE, 1967" — a book with the visible phrase: "Il n'y 
  a pas de hors-texte."
- "L'ÉCRITURE ET LA DIFFÉRENCE, 1967" — a book with the visible phrase: 
  "Le centre n'est pas le centre."
- "MARGES DE LA PHILOSOPHIE, 1972" — a book turned sideways, as if the 
  margins themselves were the front cover.
- "LA CARTE POSTALE, 1980" — a postcard partially visible, with Plato 
  and Socrates depicted, addresses crossed out.
- "SPECTRES DE MARX, 1993" — a book with a faint ghostly outline 
  hovering above its pages.
- "VOYOUS, 2003" — a late book, slightly worn, on a small side table.

ANTAGONISTIC ELEMENTS — placed at the upper outer edges:

- Upper-left, in the cool half: a small cartouche labeled "LA 
  MÉTAPHYSIQUE DE LA PRÉSENCE" — silhouettes of philosophers 
  (Plato, Husserl) gesturing toward a glowing central point labeled 
  "LE CENTRE", which is faintly crossed out with a thin red line. This 
  is the metaphysical desire for a stable origin that deconstruction 
  reads against.

- Upper-right, in the warm half: a small cartouche labeled "LA VIOLENCE 
  DE LA LETTRE" — a colonial scene of Lévi-Strauss writing names of 
  Nambikwara people in a notebook, the writing as inscription of 
  outside power. (This is a nod to the famous chapter in De la 
  Grammatologie.)

BOTTOM CORNERS — two biographical scenes in plain rectangular frames:

- Bottom left, labeled "EL-BIAR, ALGÉRIE, 1942": a 12-year-old boy in a 
  white shirt standing at the closed gate of a French lycée, holding a 
  schoolbag, the gate keeper turning him away. Behind the gate, French 
  schoolchildren in uniform; the boy is alone outside. A sense of quiet 
  exclusion. Mediterranean afternoon light. (The young Derrida excluded 
  by the Vichy antisemitic laws.)

- Bottom right, labeled "CERISY-LA-SALLE, 1980": a Norman Renaissance 
  château in summer, a long table set up under chestnut trees with 
  philosophers in animated discussion (visibly debating his work, papers 
  on the table); seated slightly apart, in a wicker chair, an older 
  Derrida with white hair listens with a slight ironic smile, holding a 
  cigarette. The philosopher observing his own deconstruction by others. 
  Warm late-afternoon light.

Bottom of the poster, in elegant serif capitals: "TOUT AUTRE EST TOUT 
AUTRE." Below it in smaller italic French: "Apprendre à vivre, enfin." 
Bottommost banner across the full width, framing the entire poster as a 
literal border-text, in small caps: "IL N'Y A PAS DE HORS-TEXTE — LA 
DIFFÉRANCE N'EST PAS, N'EXISTE PAS."

Style: late 20th-century French intellectual book cover aesthetic 
(reminiscent of Éditions de Minuit and Galilée covers from the 1960s–80s), 
photorealistic anime figure design, two figures of equal weight neither 
central nor peripheral, restrained palette divided between cool Parisian 
greys and warm Algerian ochres, vertical poster format, semi-transparent 
diagonal seam between the two halves, museum print quality, atmosphere of 
intellectual displacement and the play of trace and supplement.
